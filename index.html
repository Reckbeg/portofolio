About me

I've spent 5+ years inside HR operations across startup, technology, and education environments. My work has covered recruitment, HR administration, employee engagement, performance review, compliance, candidate follow-ups, employee records, payroll/admin recaps, policy questions, and performance-review coordination.

That background shapes how I build. I know the repeated admin work, messy spreadsheets, unclear ownership, payroll and policy pressure, and follow-up fatigue that show up when people processes depend too much on memory.

I tend to see HR problems as system design problems. If the same issue appears twice, I look for the workflow behind it. If a process depends too much on manual checking, I try to turn it into a template, automation, dashboard, or operating rhythm.

How I Think

I look for the workflow behind the problem.

When something keeps breaking, I don't just fix the task. I look for the missing structure, unclear ownership, manual handoff, or decision point that caused it, especially when a process lives in someone's memory or one fragile spreadsheet. My default move is to simplify the process, make the logic visible, and turn it into something reusable.

Workflow first

Repeated friction usually means the process is unclear, not that people are careless.

Tools support judgment

Automation should reduce coordination work, not replace context, empathy, or human decisions.

Practical before complex

A clean sheet, dashboard, checklist, or rhythm is often better than an expensive system nobody maintains.
